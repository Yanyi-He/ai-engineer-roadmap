## 04.1 Tokenization, corpora and objectives

`core` · Implement a small system and verify behavior

### Reading and scope

[Hugging Face LLM Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/llm-course/en/chapter1/1) — Public course text covering tokenizers, datasets, Transformers and training.

Tokenizers, preprocessing and causal language modeling.

### Practical evidence

Compare bilingual tokenization and inspect chat templates.

### Self-check

1. Explain how BPE is trained and how it encodes text. Why does the same Chinese passage produce very different token counts across models?
2. After fine-tuning, the model never stops generating or emits stray role markers. How do you check the chat template and special tokens?
3. Explain how perplexity relates to cross-entropy, and why perplexity can’t be compared directly across models with different tokenizers.

- MLA, sliding-window/sparse attention, multi-token prediction and MoE load balancing (including auxiliary-loss-free schemes)

3. An MoE model has many parameters but modest per-token compute. Explain routing, expert load balancing (including auxiliary-loss-free schemes) and the memory and communication costs of serving it.

## 04.5 Pretraining, post-training and capabilities

`core` · Explain mechanisms, limits and choices

Prerequisites: [03.3 ML tasks and generalization](../modules/03.md#unit-03-3), [04.1 Tokenization, corpora and objectives](../modules/04.md#unit-04-1)

### Knowledge outline

- Pretraining→SFT→preference/RL→adaptation
- Base, instruct, reasoning, embedding and reranking models
- Knowledge, reasoning, tools and long-context abilities
- Hallucination, cutoffs, OOD, sensitivity and forgetting
- Open weights, model cards, licenses and evaluation
- Scaling laws and compute-optimal training (Kaplan/Chinchilla): trading off parameters, data and compute
- Reasoning models: long chain-of-thought, RLVR training and reasoning budgets

### Reading and scope

[Hugging Face LLM Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/llm-course/en/chapter1/1) — Public course text covering tokenizers, datasets, Transformers and training.

Model types, training pipeline and limits.

### Practical evidence

Choose three candidate model types and design their evaluation.

### Self-check

1. Describe the pretraining → SFT → preference/RL pipeline. What capability does each stage change, and how do base and instruct models differ in use?
2. Under the Chinchilla scaling law, how should parameters and data be split for a fixed compute budget? Why do many open models train on far more data than “compute-optimal”?
3. On which tasks do reasoning models (long chain-of-thought, RLVR training) clearly beat standard models, and where aren’t they worth it? How do you control their reasoning budget?

## 04.7 Vision encoders and contrastive learning

`core` · Explain mechanisms, limits and choices

Prerequisites: [04.2 Transformer computation](../modules/04.md#unit-04-2)

### Knowledge outline

- ViT: patches, position encoding and image-token counts
- CLIP/SigLIP contrastive objectives, temperature and zero-shot classification
- VLM architecture from vision encoder through projector/connector to LLM, and its training stages
- Dynamic resolution, image tiling, video-frame sampling and memory/latency
- How contrastive learning underlies text embeddings and multimodal retrieval

### Reading and scope

[Hugging Face: Vision Language Models Explained](https://huggingface.co/blog/vlms) — Introductory overview of VLM components, training, evaluation and open-model choices.

Full post: VLM components, training and evaluation; read the method section of the CLIP paper for the contrastive objective.

### Practical evidence

Run zero-shot classification and image–text retrieval with an open CLIP-style model and compare the effects of resolution and prompts.

### Self-check

1. Explain how ViT turns an image into patches and tokens. How many visual tokens does a high-resolution image use, and how do you estimate it?
2. Explain CLIP’s contrastive objective and temperature, and how they enable zero-shot classification.
3. Describe a typical VLM architecture (vision encoder → projector → LLM) and its training stages. Why are text embedding models also commonly trained contrastively?
