1. Why should evaluation start with error analysis? Describe reading traces, open-coding failures, grouping them into a taxonomy and ranking by frequency × impact.
2. You realize some test questions were seen repeatedly while tuning prompts. What does that mean for your results, and how do you set up a blind test set?
3. Build a golden test set for a contract-review assistant: data sources, labeling guidelines, handling disagreement, and grouped splits by time or client.

## 11.4 Experiments, A/B tests and uncertainty

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [11.1 Tasks, labels and benchmark design](../modules/11.md#unit-11-1), [03.2 Probability, statistics and experiments](../modules/03.md#unit-03-2)

### Knowledge outline

- Strong baselines, ablations and fixed budgets
- Paired tasks, clustered sessions and randomization units
- Intervals, effects, seeds and variation
- A/B, shadow/canary and safety metrics
- Offline/online alignment, selection bias and repeated trials

### Reading and scope

[Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) — Anthropic’s statistics paper for evals: standard errors, clustered questions, paired differences and power analysis for model/system comparisons.

Full paper: standard errors, clustering, paired differences, variance reduction and power; implement bootstrap with SciPy.

### Practical evidence

Run a comparison with predeclared metrics and decision rules.

### Self-check

1. Why compare two prompts with a paired design? Should the randomization unit be the request, the session or the user?
2. In an A/B test the new version shows 2% higher satisfaction, but other features shipped at the same time. How do you decide whether the effect is credible?
3. Design a launch experiment for a model upgrade: metrics and rollback conditions at the offline, shadow, canary and full-rollout stages.

## 11.8 Environment benchmarks and verifier validation

`core` · Implement a small system and verify behavior

Prerequisites: [07.3 Harnesses and agent/environment interfaces](../modules/07.md#unit-07-3), [11.2 Layered metrics and trajectory evaluation](../modules/11.md#unit-11-2), [10.4 Untrusted data and tool safety](../modules/10.md#unit-10-4), [02.6 Cloud, containers and delivery](../modules/02.md#unit-02-6)

### Knowledge outline

- Versioned task packages implementing request, environment, stopping and scorer
- Container/dependency/data/state snapshots, isolation, reset and artifact capture
- Use an oracle or known solution to validate environment and full scoring path
- Challenge verifiers with no-op, near-correct, wrong, alternative-correct and exploit cases
- Combine code/model/human graders; verify environment outcomes separately from success claims
- Repeated attempts, replay, limits and model/agent revision matrices
- Task structures, coverage and transfer limits of Terminal-Bench, SWE-bench and Harbor
- τ-bench/τ²-bench-style tool–agent–user simulation and BFCL function-calling evaluation

### Reading and scope

[CS329Z: Evaluation Task Assignment](https://github.com/cs329z/assignment2-evals) — Public evaluation-task exercise supporting benchmark design.

HW2 examples, custom tasks, oracle, verifier and error analysis; use a small original task.

### Practical evidence

Build one task, validate known and negative solutions, inspect verifier errors, then run independent agent trials with environment/scoring/trace reports.

### Self-check

1. What components make up an environment benchmark such as SWE-bench or Terminal-Bench? Why validate the environment with an oracle solution first?
2. Your verifier passes an empty no-op submission. How do you challenge a verifier with no-op, near-correct and exploit cases?
3. Explain τ-bench’s tool–agent–user simulation, and how what it measures differs from BFCL’s function-calling evaluation.
