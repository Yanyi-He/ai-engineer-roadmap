1. Split a target JD into shared and specialist requirements. How do you tell whether a gap needs learning or just evidence?
2. Your portfolio project matches the target JD poorly. How do you adjust its scope or add evidence instead of starting over?
3. Write a capability → implementation → test → result → judgment table for your target role, covering at least three core capabilities.

## 15.2 Complete project bundle and open-source presentation

Prerequisites: [14.2 Architecture and multi-tenant products](../modules/14.md#unit-14-2), [14.6 Release, cost and ongoing operation](../modules/14.md#unit-14-6)

### Knowledge outline

- CI, issues, releases, licenses and contributing

### Reading and scope

[Full Stack LLM Bootcamp](https://fullstackdeeplearning.com/llm-bootcamp/) — Complete free recordings/materials; use 2023 content for principles and current docs for APIs.

End-to-end project, deployment and evaluation material.

### Practical evidence

Have an independent reviewer reproduce the project and request a change.

### Self-check

1. What should a project README contain to convince an interviewer within five minutes? Why include failure cases and limits?
2. An interviewer clones your repository and it won’t run. List common causes (unpinned dependencies, secrets, missing data) and how to prevent them.
3. Design reproducible evaluation commands and a results report for your project: baseline, ablations, per-case results and cost.

## 15.3 Coding and AI-application interviews

`core` · Diagnose failures, compare and repair

Prerequisites: [01.4 Practical algorithms and data structures](../modules/01.md#unit-01-4), [11.2 Layered metrics and trajectory evaluation](../modules/11.md#unit-11-2), [14.2 Architecture and multi-tenant products](../modules/14.md#unit-14-2)

### Knowledge outline

- Python/SQL/API/concurrency/database questions
- High-frequency coding patterns (hashing, two pointers, heaps, graphs/topological order, DP) under time limits
- RAG/agents/collaboration/MCP tradeoffs
- Data/metrics, experiments, cost, latency and scale
- Diagnosis, authorization, rollback and feedback

### Reading and scope

[Chip Huyen: Introduction to Machine Learning Interviews](https://huyenchip.com/ml-interviews-book/) — Free online interview book: ML role types and hiring process plus math, CS and ML-workflow question banks; supplement LLM topics from units 04–07.

Role and interview-process chapters plus CS/ML-workflow questions; self-test RAG and agent tradeoffs with units 06–07 and practise coding by 01.4 problem types.

### Practical evidence

Run a mock round of two timed coding problems, one SQL problem and a set of RAG/agent tradeoff questions; record where you stalled and study those gaps.

### Self-check

1. In 30 minutes, implement a cache with TTL and a size limit, or a streaming top-k with a heap, explaining complexity and edge cases as you go.
2. The interviewer asks what you would do about low recall in your RAG system. Give a structured diagnosis instead of a list of tricks.
3. How would you answer “when shouldn’t you use an agent?” Give a decision framework covering cost, latency, testability and risk.

## 15.4 Project defense and workplace delivery

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [15.2 Complete project bundle and open-source presentation](../modules/15.md#unit-15-2), [15.3 Coding and AI-application interviews](../modules/15.md#unit-15-3)

### Knowledge outline

- Problem, constraints, choices, results and contribution
- Failures, negative results, reproduction and hypotheses
- Discovery, estimation, reviews and documentation
- Communication, demonstrations, handoff and maintenance
- Adapting to unfamiliar requirements and diagnosing failures
- Explain evidence, assumptions and limits of a relevant paper/system; use peer review to find evaluation or implementation gaps

### Reading and scope

[Design Docs at Google](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/) — Structure of design documents (goals/non-goals, alternatives and review) for project defenses and design communication at work.

Full article: design-doc structure, alternatives and review; compare incident reviews with the postmortem chapter of the Google SRE books.

### Practical evidence

Complete a design defense, unseen failure and small change.

### Self-check

1. Present your project in three minutes: the problem, constraints, key choices, results and your personal contribution.
2. The interviewer suggests your evaluation results come from overfitting the test set. How do you respond with evidence?
3. After joining, you inherit an undocumented AI system. How would you map its current state, estimate the work and align with the team in your first two weeks?

## 15.5 LLM fundamentals interviews and hand-written implementations

`core` · Implement a small system and verify behavior

Prerequisites: [04.2 Transformer computation](../modules/04.md#unit-04-2), [04.4 Decoding and constrained generation](../modules/04.md#unit-04-4), [01.4 Practical algorithms and data structures](../modules/01.md#unit-01-4)

### Knowledge outline

- Frequent theory questions: attention complexity, KV cache, RoPE, MHA/GQA/MLA, pre-norm and RMSNorm
- Training/alignment questions: cross-entropy/perplexity, LoRA mechanics and parameter counts, SFT/DPO/PPO/GRPO differences
- Hand-write multi-head causal attention, LayerNorm/RMSNorm and a numerically stable softmax
- Hand-write top-k/top-p/temperature sampling, a BPE merge step and a LoRA linear layer
- Back-of-envelope estimates of parameters, memory and FLOPs
- Format differences between Chinese hand-coding rounds and North American live coding; narrating while coding and testing edge cases

### Reading and scope

[Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch): Code](https://github.com/rasbt/LLMs-from-scratch) — Raschka’s companion code implementing tokenization, attention, GPT, pretraining and fine-tuning chapter by chapter; ideal for hand-coding practice.

Chapters 2–7 and Appendix E (LoRA): tokenization, attention, GPT, pretraining and fine-tuning; rewrite the core components without looking.

### Practical evidence

Within 30 minutes and without references, hand-write multi-head causal attention and top-p sampling that pass your own tests; prepare spoken answers to 20 theory questions.

### Self-check

1. Hand-write multi-head causal self-attention (with masking and scaling), state its time and space complexity, and explain how the KV cache changes inference cost.
2. Explain how RMSNorm differs from LayerNorm, why pre-norm trains more stably, and why GQA shrinks the KV cache.
3. Estimate without references: how much memory do a 7B model’s FP16 weights take? Roughly how many parameters does rank-8 LoRA add when applied to every attention projection?

## 15.6 Generative-AI system design interviews

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [14.2 Architecture and multi-tenant products](../modules/14.md#unit-14-2), [11.2 Layered metrics and trajectory evaluation](../modules/11.md#unit-11-2), [05.5 Routing, caching and cost](../modules/05.md#unit-05-5)

### Knowledge outline

- Answer framework: clarify requirements and metrics → baseline → architecture → data/retrieval → model → evaluation → launch → iteration
- Platform components: context enhancement, guardrails, routers/gateways, caches, agent orchestration and observability
- Classic prompts: enterprise knowledge assistant, support-ticket agent, coding agent, document-extraction pipeline and LLM gateway
- Capacity and cost estimation: QPS, token volume, cache hits and GPU/API spend
- Failure modes and follow-ups: hallucination, privilege escalation, latency spikes and model-upgrade regressions
- Communicating tradeoffs: give a workable design first, then alternatives, costs and how to validate

### Reading and scope

[Chip Huyen: Building a Generative AI Platform](https://huyenchip.com/2024/07/25/genai-platform.html) — Builds a generative-AI platform component by component: context enhancement, guardrails, routers/gateways, caches, agents and observability; a strong skeleton for design interviews.

Full article: a platform architecture built up component by component; compare general distributed-systems parts with the System Design Primer.

### Practical evidence

In 45 minutes, design an enterprise agent with architecture and data-flow diagrams and a cost estimate, then adapt when the interviewer changes constraints.

### Self-check

1. Using the framework “clarify requirements and metrics → baseline → architecture → data/retrieval → model → evaluation → launch → iteration”, design an enterprise knowledge assistant in 45 minutes.
2. Estimate the token volume and monthly API cost for an assistant with 100,000 daily users who each hold 10 conversations a day, and explain how caching changes the result.
3. The interviewer follows up with “after launch, the model accessed data it shouldn’t have” or “quality regressed after a model upgrade”. How does your design handle each?

## 15.7 Résumé, application timing and behavioral interviews

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [15.1 JD mapping and portfolio planning](../modules/15.md#unit-15-1)

### Knowledge outline

- China campus hiring: summer internships, early and regular autumn rounds, spring rounds and referrals
- North American process: recruiter screens, online assessments, phone screens, onsite loops and referrals
- One-page résumé: write project bullets as problem–method–measured result and align them with target JD keywords
- GitHub, portfolio and technical writing: let an interviewer find evidence within five minutes
- Behavioral interviews: a STAR story bank (conflict, failure, influence, ambiguous requirements)
- Application tracking, retrospectives, offer comparison and negotiation basics

### Reading and scope

[Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/) — Free end-to-end job-search handbook: résumé, study plans, coding and behavioral interviews and negotiation.

Résumé, behavioral-interview and negotiation chapters; confirm Chinese campus-hiring dates on each target company’s careers site.

### Practical evidence

Rewrite your résumé for two target JDs, prepare six STAR stories and keep an application tracker with feedback from every round.

### Self-check

1. Rewrite one project experience as a résumé bullet in problem–method–measured-result form, aligned with a target JD’s keywords.
2. Prepare a STAR story about a failure or a disagreement with a colleague: what you did and what you learned.
3. Lay out the application timeline and interview process for your target market (China campus or experienced hiring, or North America), and design a way to track applications and review outcomes.
