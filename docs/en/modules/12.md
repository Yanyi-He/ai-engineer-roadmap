## 12.6 RLHF, PPO, GRPO and verifiable rewards

`advanced` · Explain mechanisms, limits and choices

Prerequisites: [12.2 SFT and training correctness](../modules/12.md#unit-12-2), [03.2 Probability, statistics and experiments](../modules/03.md#unit-03-2), [03.4 Neural networks and optimization](../modules/03.md#unit-03-4)

### Knowledge outline

- MDPs, policies, trajectories, returns and advantages
- SFT→reward model→policy optimization
- PPO clipping, values, KL and references
- GRPO group-relative advantages, sampling and rewards
- Verifiable/sparse rewards, hacking and stability
- GRPO reward normalization, token/sequence loss reduction, length bias and zero-variance groups
- On/off-policy learning, importance ratios, clipping and sampling-policy revisions

### Reading and scope

[RLHF Book (Nathan Lambert)](https://rlhfbook.com/) — Free online RLHF textbook: preference data, reward models, direct alignment such as DPO, PPO/GRPO and verifiable rewards with consistent notation.

RL basics, policy-gradient methods (PPO/GRPO), KL regularization and verifiable-reward chapters; check training scripts against TRL or verl.

### Practical evidence

Explain rewards, KL and success on a controlled task.

### Self-check

1. Explain the roles of clipping, the value function and the KL penalty in PPO, and how GRPO replaces the value function with group-relative advantages.
2. During GRPO with verifiable rewards, the model learns to exploit the reward (reward hacking). Give an example and explain how to detect and fix it.
3. Explain zero-variance groups, length bias and token- versus sequence-level loss reduction in GRPO, and the difference between on-policy and off-policy learning.

## 12.10 Data curation, synthesis and trace pipelines

`core` · Implement a small system and verify behavior

Prerequisites: [12.1 Training goals and data engineering](../modules/12.md#unit-12-1), [11.1 Tasks, labels and benchmark design](../modules/11.md#unit-11-1), [06.1 Ingestion and data quality](../modules/06.md#unit-06-1)

### Knowledge outline

- Failure to target distribution, sources, annotation/verification and versioned outputs
- Schemas, purpose and permissions for documents, demonstrations, preferences, traces and feedback
- Rules/quality filters, exact/near duplicates, MinHash/LSH and decontamination
- Source/language/difficulty/tail/token mixtures; split source/entity/near-duplicate groups and select processing rules using train/dev data only
- Synthetic tasks/responses/traces, teacher choice, verification, inherited errors and generation cost
- Disagreement handling, coverage audits, informative selection and targeted small evaluations
- Separate optimization/training/test data; lineage, deletion and dataset revisions
- Passing trajectories may cheat or be inefficient; retain labeled failures and separate outcome success from demonstration quality

### Reading and scope

[CS336 Lecture 14: Data pipelines](https://github.com/stanford-cs336/lectures/blob/main/lecture_14.py) — A self-contained topic on filtering, deduplication, mixing and synthetic post-training data.

Lecture 14 filtering, deduplication, mixing and post-training data; pair with CS329Z data materials and a small A4 slice.

### Practical evidence

Curate a small dataset with deduplication, grouped splits and synthetic filtering; deliver a data card, lineage and one budget-matched data ablation.

### Self-check

1. Explain how MinHash/LSH near-duplicate detection works, and why deduplication must happen before the train/test split.
2. LLM-synthesized training data raised evaluation scores, but real user feedback got worse. How do you investigate the synthetic data?
3. Design a trajectory data pipeline: passing trajectories may cheat or be inefficient, so how do you label, filter and keep failures?
