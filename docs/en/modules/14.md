## 14.1 Discovery, business metrics and design

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [00.2 From a business problem to an AI system](../modules/00.md#unit-00-2), [11.1 Tasks, labels and benchmark design](../modules/11.md#unit-11-1)

### Knowledge outline

- Discovery, workflow observation, user tasks and non-goals
- Baselines, effort, adoption, error costs and ROI
- Data availability, domain constraints and build/buy
- Work breakdown, architecture, milestones and acceptance
- Product/engineering/ML/security/business collaboration

### Reading and scope

[Full Stack LLM Bootcamp](https://fullstackdeeplearning.com/llm-bootcamp/) — Complete free recordings/materials; use 2023 content for principles and current docs for APIs.

Product, projects, evaluation, deployment and teamwork.

### Build task

Write a product brief that lets a reviewer restate success criteria.

### Self-check

1. In discovery interviews, how do you identify users’ real tasks and non-goals? Why measure the current process’s baseline first?
2. The business wants to “replace support staff with AI”. How do you estimate ROI and identify risks in data availability and error costs?
3. Write a technical proposal for an AI project: goals, non-goals, architecture, milestones, acceptance criteria and the build-versus-buy decision.

2. One tenant’s large batch job slows down every tenant. How do you fix it with quotas, isolated queues and rate limits?
3. After a document update, the database, vector index and caches fall out of sync. Design a consistency strategy and a repair process.

### Self-check

2. Users often click “approve” without reading. How would you redesign the approval UI so risky actions actually get reviewed?
3. Design the interaction when an agent fails or can’t finish: how to tell the user, offer a way to edit, and hand off to a human.

## 14.4 Reliability, SLOs and incidents

`core` · Diagnose failures, compare and repair

Prerequisites: [02.6 Cloud, containers and delivery](../modules/02.md#unit-02-6), [11.5 Tracing, logs and production diagnosis](../modules/11.md#unit-11-5)

### Knowledge outline

- SLIs/SLOs, error budgets and critical paths
- Limits, budgets, deadlines, retries and fallback
- Redelivery, crashes, queues and dependency failure
- Backup/restore and schema/model/index rollback
- Alerts, on-call, runbooks, incidents and improvements
- Application load testing: simulate realistic request mixes with Locust or k6 and verify that queuing, rate limits, timeouts and degradation behave as designed

### Reading and scope

[Google SRE Books](https://sre.google/books/) — Official complete free SRE books on SLOs, alerts, incidents and capacity.

SLOs, monitoring, overload, incidents and postmortems.

### Build task

Drill three failure classes and write an incident review.

### Self-check

1. Explain SLIs, SLOs and error budgets, and define two SLIs with matching SLOs for an AI Q&A service.
2. Your model provider has a major outage. How should your service degrade (backup model, cache, read-only mode), and how do you notify users?
3. Outline a postmortem: timeline, impact, root cause, fixes and follow-ups. Why should a postmortem be blameless?

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

### Build task

### Self-check

1. Threat-model an agent with access to internal systems: what are the assets, attackers, entry points and exfiltration paths?
2. A user asks for their personal data to be deleted. What happens to copies in logs, traces, vector indexes and training data?
3. Before launching an AI feature in a domain such as healthcare or finance, which domain reviews, responsibility assignments and escalation paths are needed?

## 14.6 Release, cost and ongoing operation

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [14.4 Reliability, SLOs and incidents](../modules/14.md#unit-14-4), [11.6 Regression, versions and releases](../modules/11.md#unit-11-6), [05.5 Routing, caching and cost](../modules/05.md#unit-05-5)

### Knowledge outline

- Traceable releases across model/prompt/tools/index/data
- Automated/manual evaluation, shadow/canary and rollback
- Inference/training/storage/observability cost and budgets
- Adoption, success, time saved and corrections
- Maintenance, upgrades, handoff and documentation
- Environment promotion (sandbox → dev → QA → UAT → prod), architecture decision records, API specifications and runbooks
- Feature flags and progressive delivery: roll out by tenant, user or percentage, compare metrics, then expand, roll back or retire flags (OpenFeature and similar)

### Reading and scope

[What We’ve Learned From A Year of Building with LLMs](https://applied-llms.org/) — Practitioner-written lessons on LLM products; tactical, operational and strategic sections cover evaluation, version pinning, cost and team roles.

The operational section (data, model pinning, product and people) and tactical evaluation/monitoring advice; build the release pipeline with GitHub Actions docs.

### Build task

Release a reversible change with operation/cost evidence.

### Self-check

1. A release may change the model, prompt, tools, index and data at once. How do you keep every release traceable and reversible?
2. The monthly model bill suddenly doubles. How do you find the cause from traces and usage data (retries, context growth, traffic growth)?
3. Design post-launch operating metrics (adoption, success rate, time saved and human-correction rate) and how to collect each.

## 14.7 Risk tiers, privacy boundaries and safety evaluation

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [14.5 Security, privacy and domain governance](../modules/14.md#unit-14-5), [11.3 Rules, humans and model judges](../modules/11.md#unit-11-3), [11.8 Environment benchmarks and verifier validation](../modules/11.md#unit-11-8)

### Knowledge outline

- Threat models covering assets, principals, sources, actions, attackers and egress
- Direct/indirect injection, poisoned retrieval, cross-tenant leaks and composed-tool risks
- Authorized access versus appropriate disclosure; contextual privacy, inferences and minimization
- Separate guardrails, permissions, human intervention and isolation responsibilities
- Scenario attacks, attack success, false blocks, utility and long-trajectory risk
- Escalation, evidence, revocation, recovery and release risk acceptance

### Reading and scope

[Stanford CS329Z: Engineering AI Agents](https://cs329z.stanford.edu/) — Agent-engineering spine connecting frameworks, data, optimization, evaluation and safety through released material.

Published 18 November safety material; connect 10.4 execution controls and 11.8 verifier validation. Include the AgentDojo tool-result injection and contextual-disclosure examples.

### Build task

Threat-model the product, evaluate benign/adversarial scenarios and false blocks, then reproduce and verify one repair.

### Self-check

1. Explain the difference between being authorized to access information and it being appropriate to disclose. Give an example of data an agent can read but shouldn’t reveal.
2. The retrieval corpus is poisoned and the assistant serves a malicious link. Analyze the attack path and the separate roles of guardrails, permissions and human intervention.
3. Design scenario-based safety evaluations: how do you measure attack success, false blocks and utility together? Who accepts the residual risk at launch?

## 14.8 Event-driven and proactive interaction

`advanced` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [14.5 Security, privacy and domain governance](../modules/14.md#unit-14-5), [07.4 State, persistence and recovery](../modules/07.md#unit-07-4), [02.4 Caches, queues and workers](../modules/02.md#unit-02-4)

### Knowledge outline

- Request/event/scheduled/background triggers versus continuous observation
- Declared and inferred preferences, confidence, provenance and correction
- When to suggest, notify, ask or act; frequency, interruption costs and authorization
- Deduplication, stale events, races, cancellation and compute budgets
- User controls, revocation, quiet modes, privacy and explainable action records
- Measure acceptance, value, false triggers, interruption and long-term trust

### Reading and scope

[Creating General User Models from Computer Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10831) — Complete free paper on proactive interaction, correctable inferences and privacy tradeoffs.

Published GUM paper and event-driven foundations; revisit after the upcoming2 December class.

### Build task

Design a disableable event-triggered assistant; evaluate duplicates, stale/false triggers and revocation with simulated events.

### Self-check

1. When should a proactive agent suggest, notify, ask or act on its own? How do you weigh interruption cost against value?
2. After launching proactive reminders, users keep turning notifications off. How do you analyze false triggers, duplicate events and timing?
3. Design user controls over proactive behavior: declared preferences, revocation, quiet modes and explainable action records.

## 14.9 AI regulation, filing and compliance

`advanced` · Explain mechanisms, limits and choices

Prerequisites: [14.5 Security, privacy and domain governance](../modules/14.md#unit-14-5)

### Knowledge outline

- China: scope of the Interim Measures for Generative AI Services and provider obligations
- Algorithm filing, generative-AI service (model) filing and the engineering evidence for security assessments
- Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Content: explicit/implicit labels and metadata implementation
- PIPL, the Data Security Law and cross-border transfer: minimization, consent and calling overseas models
- EU AI Act risk tiers, GPAI obligations and transparency duties; NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001
- Model/data licenses, vendor data terms, output copyright and audit trails

### Reading and scope

[Interim Measures for Generative AI Services (生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法)](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm) — Regulation on generative-AI services issued by the CAC and six other agencies (2023); obligations and the basis for filing when serving the public in China.

Full text of the measures; read the labeling measures for marking requirements and the NIST AI RMF and EU AI Act overview for North America/EU. Engineering orientation only, not legal advice.

### Build task

Draft a compliance checklist for a public generative feature (labeling, log retention, cross-border data and filing evidence) and mark the items that need legal review.

### Self-check

1. Summarize the main obligations that China’s Interim Measures for Generative AI Services place on public-facing providers, and how algorithm filing differs from model filing.
2. Under China’s Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Content, where does your product need explicit and implicit labels, and how do you implement them?
3. What personal-information and cross-border data issues arise when a China-based business calls an overseas model API? What does the EU AI Act require of general-purpose models and high-risk systems?
