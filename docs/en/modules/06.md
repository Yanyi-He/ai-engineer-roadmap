## 06.1 Ingestion and data quality

`core` · Diagnose failures, compare and repair

Prerequisites: [02.2 SQL, modeling and indexes](../modules/02.md#unit-02-2)

### Knowledge outline

- Connectors, incremental sync, provenance and data cards
- PDF/HTML/Office/OCR, layout, tables and encoding
- Cleaning, hashes, document/chunk IDs and revisions
- Structural/semantic chunks, overlap and metadata
- Batching, replay, idempotent indexes and deletion
- Choosing parsers (Docling, MinerU, PaddleOCR, Unstructured) or cloud document-intelligence services (Azure Document Intelligence, AWS Textract), and evaluating parse quality by document type
- Enterprise data platforms: Spark/Databricks batch processing, Airflow orchestration, lakehouse table formats (Delta/Iceberg) and lineage

### Reading and scope

[Docling Documentation](https://docling-project.github.io/docling/) — Open-source document conversion: PDF/Office/HTML, layout, tables, OCR and structure-aware chunking; shows how parsing quality affects retrieval.

Supported formats, the PDF layout/table/OCR pipeline and chunking; compare Chinese-document parsing with MinerU.

### Build task

Build replayable ingestion for a source with revisions/deletion.

### Self-check

1. Compare fixed-length, structure-based and semantic chunking. What do overlap and metadata contribute?
2. Users report that answers cite an old document that was deleted. How do you design document IDs, revisions and incremental sync so deletions actually take effect?
3. For scanned PDFs, financial reports with complex tables and web pages, how would you choose parsers and evaluate parse quality?

## 06.2 Embeddings and similarity

`core` · Diagnose failures, compare and repair

Prerequisites: [03.1 Linear algebra and numerical basics](../modules/03.md#unit-03-1), [04.1 Tokenization, corpora and objectives](../modules/04.md#unit-04-1)

### Knowledge outline

- Bi-encoders, query/document representations and objectives
- Cosine/dot/L2, normalization, dimension and distribution
- Languages, domains, prefixes and maximum length
- Batching, caches, migration and rebuilding
- Positives, hard negatives, similarity versus relevance
- Choosing embedding models (BGE, Qwen3-Embedding and others) with MTEB/C-MTEB and your own data; Matryoshka dimensions and quantization
- Fine-tuning embedding/reranker models with hard negatives and evaluating them

### Reading and scope

[Sentence Transformers: Retrieve and Rerank](https://www.sbert.net/examples/sentence_transformer/applications/retrieve_rerank/README.html) — Understand bi-encoder retrieval versus cross-encoder reranking tradeoffs.

Semantic search, model selection and retrieval evaluation.

### Build task

Compare two embeddings and a lexical baseline with the same qrels.

### Self-check

1. Explain the training objective of bi-encoder embeddings, and why semantic similarity is not the same as relevance to a question.
2. After switching embedding models, retrieval got worse in some domains. How do you evaluate, migrate and rebuild the index without disrupting production?
3. How do you choose an embedding model: MTEB/C-MTEB scores, evaluation on your own data, dimensions and cost? When is fine-tuning with hard negatives worth it?

Measure Recall@k/latency tradeoffs across index parameters.

2. Explain how HNSW works and its key parameters (M, ef). How would you tune it when recall drops or memory grows too large?
3. For 10 million documents, multiple tenants and hybrid search, compare pgvector, Milvus and Elasticsearch.

### Knowledge outline

### Build task

### Self-check

1. Explain how RRF fuses BM25 and vector results, and why you shouldn’t simply add their scores.
2. Adding a reranker didn’t improve answer quality. How do you tell whether the problem is in recall (the right document was never retrieved) or in reranking?
3. Compare bi-encoders, cross-encoders and ColBERT-style late interaction on quality, latency and storage, and set a candidate budget.

2. A multi-hop question (find company A’s CEO, then their education) fails retrieval. How would you design the retrieval flow while bounding steps and cost?
3. Explain contextual retrieval and parent-child (small-to-big) chunking, and how you would show experimentally that they help.

## 06.6 Grounded answers and citations

`core` · Diagnose failures, compare and repair

Prerequisites: [06.3 Lexical, vector and ANN indexes](../modules/06.md#unit-06-3), [05.3 Structured output and tool execution](../modules/05.md#unit-05-3), [11.1 Tasks, labels and benchmark design](../modules/11.md#unit-11-1)

### Knowledge outline

- Context assembly, order, duplicates and budgets
- Claim-level citations, source spans and revisions
- Support/contradiction/insufficiency and qualifiers
- Separate answer, evidence and citation correctness
- Abstention, clarification, stale/conflicting knowledge and UX

### Reading and scope

[ALCE: Enabling LLMs to Generate Text with Citations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627) — Benchmark paper for cited generation; defines citation recall/precision and separates answer correctness, evidential support and citation quality.

Citation recall/precision definitions, evaluation setup and failure analysis; pair claim-level labels with Ragas faithfulness.

### Build task

Label claims/evidence and measure false support and coverage.

### Self-check

1. Explain why answer correctness, faithfulness and citation correctness are three different things, with an example that satisfies only one of each.
2. Two retrieved documents contradict each other (old and new policy), and the model cites only the old one. How do you handle this in context assembly and prompting?
3. Design an abstention mechanism: when should the answer be “the sources don’t cover this”, and how do you measure abstention and false-abstention rates?

Compare SQL, documents and a hybrid analytics workflow.

### Self-check

3. An assistant must answer both “what does the policy say” and “what were last month’s sales”. Design routing and result merging across documents, SQL and a graph.

## 06.8 Operating and maintaining RAG

`core` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [06.6 Grounded answers and citations](../modules/06.md#unit-06-6), [11.2 Layered metrics and trajectory evaluation](../modules/11.md#unit-11-2), [02.5 Identity, authorization and tenancy](../modules/02.md#unit-02-5)

### Knowledge outline

- Qrels, Recall/nDCG/MRR and generation metrics
- Authorization canaries, temporal slices and leakage
- Refresh, deletion, invalidation and migration
- Failure feedback and model upgrades
- Quality, p95, resources and cost per success

### Reading and scope

[Jason Liu: Systematically Improving Your RAG](https://jxnl.co/writing/2024/05/22/systematically-improving-your-rag/) — Method for improving RAG: synthetic questions as a baseline, retrieval metrics, query segmentation, user feedback and targeted fixes.

Full article: synthetic-question baselines, retrieval metrics, query segmentation and feedback loops; check index rebuilds, deletion and rollback against pgvector.

### Build task

Drill updates, revoked access and index rollback.

### Self-check

1. Explain how Recall@k, MRR and nDCG differ, and how labeling granularity (document versus chunk) in qrels affects them.
2. RAG answer quality degrades gradually after launch. List possible causes (document updates, stale indexes, a shifting question mix) and how you would monitor them.
3. Design authorization-canary tests that continuously verify that no role, tenant or time slice can retrieve content it isn’t allowed to see.

## 06.9 Visual document retrieval and long-context tradeoffs

`advanced` · Design under constraints and defend tradeoffs

Prerequisites: [06.4 Hybrid retrieval and reranking](../modules/06.md#unit-06-4), [05.6 Vision, documents and multimodal input](../modules/05.md#unit-05-6)

### Knowledge outline

- Retrieving page images directly: ColPali/multi-vector late interaction versus OCR pipelines
- Retrieval and answering over tables, charts, scans and layout
- Long-context windows versus RAG: cost, latency, position bias and context rot
- Hybrid strategies: retrieve-then-read-whole-document, cached long documents and hierarchical summaries
- Storage/latency costs of multi-vector indexes and how to evaluate them

### Reading and scope

[ColPali: Efficient Document Retrieval with Vision Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01449) — Paper on multi-vector retrieval over page images and the ViDoRe benchmark; compare with OCR+text pipelines.

Method and ViDoRe evaluation sections; combine long-context tradeoffs with 05.4 and 05.10.

### Build task

On PDFs with tables/charts, compare OCR+text retrieval, page-image retrieval and long-context reading for quality, latency and cost.

### Self-check

1. Explain how ColPali retrieves page images directly (multi-vector late interaction) and how it compares with an OCR pipeline.
2. Once a model supports a million-token context, do you still need RAG? Argue from cost, latency, position bias and permissions.
3. Design a hybrid strategy: retrieve to locate documents, then read them whole or use cached long documents. How would you evaluate it against plain RAG?
