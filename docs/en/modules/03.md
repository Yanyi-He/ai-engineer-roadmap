## 03.1 Linear algebra and numerical basics

`core` · Implement a small system and verify behavior

Prerequisites: [01.1 Python language and data model](../modules/01.md#unit-01-1)

### Knowledge outline

- NumPy vectors, matrices, tensors, shapes, broadcasting and matrix products
- Dot products, norms, cosine and similarity
- Derivatives, chain rule, gradients and Jacobians
- Floating point, overflow and numerical stability
- Parameter, activation and compute estimates

### Reading and scope

[Dive into Deep Learning](https://d2l.ai/) — Complete interactive textbook; select optimization, attention and Transformer chapters.

Preliminaries: linear algebra, calculus and automatic differentiation.

### Practical evidence

Compute and verify softmax, cosine and a gradient update.

### Self-check

1. Explain how cosine similarity relates to the dot product. What changes once vectors are normalized?
2. A softmax produces NaN or inf. What are the likely causes? Write a numerically stable softmax.
3. Estimate the FLOPs and output memory of multiplying a (batch, seq, d_model) tensor by a (d_model, 4·d_model) matrix.

## 03.2 Probability, statistics and experiments

`core` · Diagnose failures, compare and repair

Prerequisites: [03.1 Linear algebra and numerical basics](../modules/03.md#unit-03-1)

### Knowledge outline

- Conditional probability, Bayes, moments and distributions
- Sampling units, dependence, bias/variance and intervals
- Tests, effect size, power and multiple comparisons
- Paired comparisons, cluster bootstrap and grouped/time splits
- Calibration, coverage, selection bias and causality
- Entropy, cross-entropy, KL divergence and mutual information; how MLE/MAP relate to common losses

### Reading and scope

[OpenIntro Statistics](https://www.openintro.org/book/os/) — Free open statistics textbook: sampling, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, power and paired data for experimental judgment.

Probability and distributions, foundations for inference, inference for numerical data (including paired data) and power; implement bootstrap with SciPy; read the information-theory appendix in D2L.

### Practical evidence

Compare systems on paired tasks and report uncertainty.

### Self-check

1. Explain how cross-entropy, KL divergence and maximum-likelihood estimation relate. Why do classifiers use cross-entropy as their loss?
2. A new prompt raises accuracy from 70% to 76% on 50 test cases. How do you decide whether the gain is real (paired comparison, confidence interval, sample size)?
3. Turns from the same user’s conversations ended up in both the training and test sets. What bias does that cause, and how should you split the data and compute intervals?

3. For a classifier that decides whether a ticket needs a human, design the baseline, data splits and a threshold tied to business cost.

`core` · Diagnose failures, compare and repair

Prerequisites: [03.1 Linear algebra and numerical basics](../modules/03.md#unit-03-1), [03.3 ML tasks and generalization](../modules/03.md#unit-03-3)

### Reading and scope

[Dive into Deep Learning](https://d2l.ai/) — Complete interactive textbook; select optimization, attention and Transformer chapters.

MLPs, backpropagation, optimizers and computation.

### Practical evidence

Train a small network and diagnose overfitting/gradient failure.

### Self-check

1. Explain how backpropagation and automatic differentiation work. How does AdamW differ from Adam in handling weight decay?
2. Training loss doesn’t decrease from the start, or quickly turns NaN. What do you check, and in what order (learning rate, initialization, gradient clipping, data)?
3. Memory fits batch size 4 but you need an effective batch of 32. Explain how to implement gradient accumulation and how the learning-rate schedule must account for it.

## 03.5 PyTorch training engineering

`core` · Implement a small system and verify behavior

Prerequisites: [03.4 Neural networks and optimization](../modules/03.md#unit-03-4), [01.5 Testing, debugging and profiling](../modules/01.md#unit-01-5)

### Knowledge outline

- Tensors, devices, dtypes, modules and parameters
- Datasets/loaders, collate, padding and sampling
- Forward/backward, masks and loss reduction
- AMP, checkpoints, resume, logs and configuration
- Memory profiling, OOM diagnosis and minimal reproductions
- Strides, views/reshape, contiguous layout, broadcasting and einsum/einops; copies and memory

### Reading and scope

[PyTorch: Learn the Basics](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/beginner/basics/intro.html) — Official complete beginner flow from tensors to training and saving.

Tensors, autograd, datasets/loaders, build/optimize/save.

### Practical evidence

Write a training script that resumes from a checkpoint.

### Self-check

1. Explain the difference between view, reshape and contiguous. When does view raise an error?
2. Training runs out of memory midway. How do you find whether activations, optimizer state or a particular batch is responsible, and build a minimal reproduction?
3. Sketch a training loop with mixed precision, checkpointing and resume, and list the state that must be restored on resume.
